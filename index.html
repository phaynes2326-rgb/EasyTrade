03Grinder

Get the right grinder

The grinder matters more than the brewer. A burr grinder gives consistent particle size; blade grinders chop unevenly and produce muddy, uneven extraction no matter how good your beans are.

If you only buy one piece of gear — make it a decent burr grinder. It improves every method above.
04Ratio

Learn the coffee-to-water ratio

Most methods work well starting around a 1:16 ratio — 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water. Adjust from there to taste.

Stronger

Move toward 1:14 — less water per gram of coffee.

Lighter

Move toward 1:18 — more water per gram of coffee.

05Grind size

Match your grind size to your method

  • Coarse — French press, cold brew.
  • Medium — drip machines, some pour-overs.
  • Fine — pour-over cones like the V60.
  • Extra fine — espresso.

Too fine for your method = bitter, over-extracted. Too coarse = weak, sour, under-extracted.

06Counter setup

Build your counter setup

You don't need much space. A small dedicated corner keeps the habit easy to maintain.

Essentials

Grinder, kettle, brewer, a scale, mugs.

Nice to have

Gooseneck kettle, timer, storage canister with a seal.

07First brew

Dial in your first brew

  1. Weigh your coffee and water using your target ratio.
  2. Heat water to just off the boil (around 200°F / 93°C).
  3. Grind right before brewing, not ahead of time.
  4. Brew, taste, and note what you'd change.

Adjust one variable at a time — grind size, ratio, or water temperature — so you know what caused the change in taste.

08Troubleshoot

Troubleshoot common mistakes

Tastes sour

Usually under-extracted — grind finer or brew longer.

Tastes bitter

Usually over-extracted — grind coarser or brew shorter.

Tastes weak

Not enough coffee for the water — tighten your ratio.
