08MT5 Basics

Get oriented in MetaTrader 5

Spend time exploring these key areas before placing any trades:

📊 Chart Window

The main area showing price charts. Tap any symbol to view its chart. Pinch to zoom (mobile) or use mouse scroll (desktop). Try different timeframes: M1 (1 minute), H1 (1 hour), D1 (daily).

📈 Market Watch / Quotes

Real-time price list for all instruments. Mobile: Tap "Quotes" tab. Desktop: View → Market Watch. Add symbols with the "+" button. Tap a symbol to open its chart.

💼 Trade Tab / Terminal

Shows your open positions, balance, equity, and margin. Check here to see how your demo trades are performing. Also displays trade history.

🔧 Navigator

Switch between accounts, access indicators, and manage trading tools. Use this to add technical indicators to your charts.

Practice placing a trade:

  1. Tap a symbol in Quotes → Select "Trade" or "New Order"
  2. Choose Market Order (executes immediately)
  3. Set volume to 0.01 lots (smallest size)
  4. Set a Stop Loss (e.g., 20 pips away)
  5. Click Buy or Sell
  6. Watch it in the Trade tab
  7. Close it manually after a few minutes

Do this 5-10 times until it feels routine. Get comfortable with the mechanics before worrying about which direction to trade.

09Trading Vocabulary

Learn the essential vocabulary

These terms show up constantly. Understanding them cold before you trade live matters more than any strategy or signal.

Spread
The gap between the buy and sell price — effectively the cost of entering a trade. Lower spreads = lower trading costs.
Leverage
Borrowed exposure that multiplies both gains and losses. Higher leverage means you can control larger positions with less capital, but also means higher risk of losing your full balance faster.
Pip
The smallest standard price move in a currency pair (usually the 4th decimal place). Used to measure profit/loss. Example: EUR/USD moving from 1.1000 to 1.1001 is a 1-pip move.
Lot Size
The size of your trade. Standard lot = 100,000 units. Mini lot = 10,000 units. Micro lot (0.01) = 1,000 units. Start with micro lots (0.01) when learning.
Stop Loss (SL)
An order that automatically closes a trade at a set loss level, limiting your downside. Always use this. It protects you from catastrophic losses.
Take Profit (TP)
An order that automatically closes a trade when it reaches your profit target. Locks in gains without needing to monitor constantly.
Margin
The amount of money required to open and maintain a leveraged position. Your broker holds this as collateral.
Margin Call
A broker demand for more funds when your account can't cover open losses. Can result in forced closure of positions. Avoid by managing risk properly.
Equity
Your account balance plus/minus any unrealized profit or loss from open positions. This is your actual account value at any moment.
Candlestick
A chart bar showing open, high, low, and close prices for a time period. Green/white = price went up. Red/black = price went down.

11Risk Management

Manage risk before you manage trades

Most new traders lose money not because they picked the wrong direction, but because of how much they risked per trade. A few habits that matter more than any signal or strategy:

Core risk principles:

  • Never fund a live account with money you can't afford to lose completely. Trading can result in total loss of capital.
  • Risk only 1-2% of your account per trade. If you have $1,000, risk no more than $10-20 per trade. This keeps you in the game even after a string of losses.
  • Always use a Stop Loss. Decide your maximum loss before opening a trade, and set a stop-loss to enforce it automatically.
  • Start with micro lots (0.01). Even on a live account, trade the smallest size possible while you're learning.
  • Don't chase losses. If you have a bad trade, resist the urge to "make it back" with a bigger position. That's how accounts blow up.

Be skeptical of guarantees:

  • No legitimate trading approach can guarantee profits or promise fast returns.
  • Be cautious of "signal groups" or paid programs that combine trading with recruiting others for commissions — that structure carries separate risks worth researching independently.
  • If something sounds too good to be true, it probably is.

The 5-trade rule

Before increasing your position size, complete at least 5 consecutive trades following your risk rules (Stop Loss on every trade, max 1-2% risk). If you can't do that, you're not ready to scale up.
